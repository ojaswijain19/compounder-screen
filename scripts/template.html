How the score works

Each stock gets a percentile rank (0–100) against all screened names on every metric below. Metrics are averaged into five factors, and the factors are combined with the weights you set. A missing metric is skipped. A factor with no data at all counts as the 35th percentile, so thin data costs a little rather than nothing.

Growth
TTM revenue growth year on year, and 3-year revenue CAGR from annual filings.
Quality
ROIC (operating income × 0.79 ÷ equity + debt − cash), FCF margin, operating margin. Banks and insurers use ROE only.
Value
FCF yield and earnings yield on current market cap. US-dollar reporters only.
Balance sheet
Net debt ÷ EBITDA, lower is better. Net cash ranks best.
Trend
6-month price return and distance from the 200-day moving average.

Read before you act

  • All figures are GAAP (or IFRS) trailing twelve months from 10-K/10-Q XBRL data. One-off items, write-downs and investment gains are not adjusted out. Alphabet's net margin, for example, includes gains on private stakes.
  • Foreign filers (20-F: ASML, BABA, NU, XPEV, LI, PDD and others) report annually, so their figures are the last fiscal year. Non-USD reporters show no valuation metrics.
  • "Stale" means the latest period in SEC data ends more than 200 days ago. That's usually a tagging change or annual-only filer, not missing reports. These are hidden by default.
  • A ≈ mark means an approximation: pre-tax income used where no operating income is tagged, or net debt ÷ EBIT where D&A isn't tagged.
  • FCF is operating cash flow minus capex, before stock-based compensation. SBC as % of revenue is shown in each stock's detail so you can judge the dilution.
  • This is a starting list for research. Every name still needs the filings, the call and the thesis check.
